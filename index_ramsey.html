NV Magnetometry — Ramsey Interferometry

The sequence a real NV magnetometer runs to measure a field. A π/2 pulse tips the spin onto the equator; during a protected free-evolution window τ it precesses at the detuning rate, banking phase φ=δτ; a second π/2 pulse converts that phase into population you can read. Sweep τ and the readout oscillates as cos(δτ) — the fringe frequency is the field.

The whole thing is a two-gate quantum circuit with a phase-accumulating gap. Unlike continuous Rabi driving, the microwave is off during τ — the spin is a free-running clock whose rate is the field. That protected window is why Ramsey resolves the field far more finely than a Rabi fit.

Bloch sphere — driven by the sequence

Watch the three beats: the first π/2 lays the vector onto the equator (green). During free evolution it sweeps the equator (cyan) — that sweep angle is the banked phase. The second π/2 folds the equator back toward the poles; where the vector had rotated to decides how far north or south it lands, and that height is what you measure.

Ramsey fringes — P(|0⟩) vs free-evolution time τ

P(τ) = ½(1+cos δτ)·e^(−τ/T₂*) T₂* envelope current τ
The fringe spacing is set entirely by the detuning: bigger field → faster precession → tighter fringes. Reading the fringe frequency and inverting gives B. The red envelope is dephasing — wait past it and the phase information is gone, which caps the useful τ.
Physics. Ramsey readout P(|0⟩)=½(1+cos(δτ))·e^(−τ/T₂*); accumulated phase φ=δτ. Detuning δ = 2π·(f_drive − f₊), with Zeeman resonance f₊ = 2.87 GHz + γB, γ≈28 MHz/mT. Equivalent circuit: |0⟩ – RY(π/2) – [free phase δτ] – RY(π/2) – measure. Closed-form, computed client-side, no server. The coherent evolution is exact; T₂* dephasing is the one open-system effect, added as the fringe envelope.
Terrain height is an illustrative stand-in for crustal field magnitude; the spin physics is faithful to the two-level NV model.
