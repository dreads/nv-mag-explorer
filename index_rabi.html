NV Magnetometry — Rabi Oscillations & the Zeeman Effect

Slide a magnet toward or away from a fixed NV sensor. Distance sets the local field B via a real inverse-cube dipole law. That field shifts the NV center's spin resonance (Zeeman effect), which detunes the microwave drive and reshapes the Rabi oscillation — the quantum signal a real magnetometer reads to sense field strength.

The generalized Rabi formula: P(t) = (Ω₀²/Ω²)·½(1−cos Ωt), with effective frequency Ω = √(Ω₀² + δ²). Detuning does two things at once — it speeds up the wobble and shrinks its amplitude (the spin can no longer fully flip). Watch both as you drive off the resonant sweet spot.

Bloch sphere

The spin state as a vector. On resonance it sweeps the full poles (|0⟩→|±1⟩). Off resonance the axis tilts and it traces a tight cone — never quite reaching the south pole.

Energy levels

Zero field: m=±1 are degenerate at 2.87 GHz. The field pries them apart by ±γB (γ≈28 MHz/mT). The microwave (amber) is fixed; the gap moves — that mismatch is the detuning.

ODMR — fluorescence vs microwave frequency sweep

What the photodetector actually sees: red fluorescence dips where the microwave hits a spin resonance. The field splits one dip into two (m=−1 and m=+1). The amber line is your fixed drive frequency — the horizontal distance from it to the nearest dip is the detuning you feel in the Rabi trace.
